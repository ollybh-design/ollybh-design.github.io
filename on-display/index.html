On Display

A shoebox that becomes the stand for the shoes inside it.

Plenty of people keep the box, and a lot of pairs are bought to keep rather than to wear. This box earns its place on the shelf. Lift the platform inside, fold down two supports and the box stands on its own lid as a tilted plinth, showing the pair like a shop display.

Still a shoeboxBase and lid like any shoebox. It packs, stacks and ships the same way, with the platform lying flat above the pair.
Lift, fold, lockThe platform is hinged at the front and lifts by a pull tab at the back. Two supports fold down from under it and lock into slots on the side ledges at 12°.
Stands on its lidThe lid becomes a plinth, so the whole box turns into a small display piece for a shelf or a desk.
Three flat partsBase, platform and lid in E flute board. Glued corners like any shoebox, no plastic, and the supports are cut from the platform blank.

Flat pack

Three flat parts.

Each part is a single die cut blank of E flute board. Base and lid are built like a standard two piece shoebox, with glued corner flaps. The long walls of the base fold over at the rim to make the two ledges, and the platform's hinge flap is glued inside the base front wall, so base and platform travel together, flat.

1 BaseFloor, two end walls with glued corner flaps, and two long walls. Each long wall folds over at the rim and down inside to make a 16 mm ledge; the inner wall locks into the floor with tabs. From inside it reads as one wall with a ledge, as in the renders.
2 Display platformA top layer with folded edges, a pull tab on its rear edge and an under layer folded back from the rear. Two supports hang from the under layer; each has a foot with two tabs that drop into slots in the ledge.
3 LidA standard telescoping shoebox lid with glued corners. On display the box stands on it, top side up, as a plinth.

How it is folded

From flat parts to a packed, branded box. Base and lid go up by hand or on the same erecting equipment used for other two piece shoeboxes.

Manufacturing

Made on the same lines as shoeboxes today.

No new process. Every part is printed, die cut and creased like a standard corrugated shoebox, with one extra blank for the platform.

Specification

  • Board: E flute single wall, about 1.6 mm, printed one side.
  • Print: flexo or litho laminate on the outside faces, in any brand's artwork. The platform stays kraft with a printed toe line.
  • Tooling: three cutting dies per size (base, platform, lid).
  • Glue: corner flaps on the base and lid, as on any two piece shoebox, plus the platform's hinge flap inside the base front wall.
  • No plastic, no magnets, no separate stand. Everything stays attached, so nothing gets lost.

Packing the pair

  • Base: end walls up, corner flaps glued, long walls folded up and over to form the ledges. The platform swings down onto the ledges.
  • Lift the platform, pack the pair with its tissue underneath, lower it, put the lid on.
  • Packed, it is the same shape as a normal two piece shoebox and stacks the same way.

Why the extra board is worth it

  • It replaces a second product: no separate acrylic case or display stand to make, ship and buy.
  • It is a box people keep, so the board stays in use on a shelf instead of going straight into the recycling.
  • It is still all paper, with no plastic, so it recycles like any shoebox at the end.

Branding

Any brand. Showcased by adidas.

The structure carries no logo of its own. The outside takes any artwork, from plain kraft to a full collab print, while the kraft platform keeps the pair the hero.

Why it works

Some pairs are bought to be looked at.

For a lot of buyers the box is part of the product: it is kept, stacked and shown. On Display makes that the point of the box, and keeps the brand in the room long after the unboxing.
